# US cashback service comparison

Research date: 2026-09-02. This note uses only first-party program terms, privacy notices, help centers, and official browser-extension listings. Offers, merchant participation, exclusions, and redemption rules can change, so they should be checked again before a large purchase.

## Short answer

There is no universally best service. A service can have the highest rate at one merchant and no eligible offer at another, while tracking exclusions and payout delays vary by transaction. For an occasional shopper who values privacy, the best default is to compare offers only after deciding what to buy, choose one service, and use that service's website link in a dedicated `Shopping` profile without installing its extension.

TopCashback is the strongest starting point among these six when actual cash and low payout friction matter: it supports a website click-through, and ACH, PayPal, and Venmo have no minimum. BeFrugal is a similar cash-paying alternative with a one-cent minimum for its electronic methods. Neither is a permanent rate winner. Rakuten remains reasonable when its offer is better or its Amex/Bilt options are useful, but cash is paid quarterly and requires $5.01. RetailMeNot's $5.01 threshold and inactivity fee make it less attractive for rare use. PayPal Honey now pays points rather than withdrawable cash. Capital One Shopping is the weakest privacy fit because its documented desktop earning flow relies on a broadly permissioned extension and rewards are gift cards only.

None of the six officially names Zen as supported, and Rakuten explicitly says third-party browsers are not guaranteed. Firefox is therefore the compatibility-first transaction browser. Zen nevertheless supports Firefox-style profiles through `about:profiles`; a dedicated Zen `Shopping` profile is the better UX-first choice when keeping daily and purchasing work in one browser matters more than official portal support. Start with a low-value transaction and retain Firefox as a fallback. [Zen profile guide](https://docs.zen-browser.app/guides/manage-profiles), [Rakuten supported browsers](https://www.rakuten.com/help/article/supported-browsers-360002101188)

## Analysis and recommendation

The following points are recommendations or inferences from the documented facts, not promises made by the providers.

### Preferred workflow

1. Research products normally in Zen with its daily privacy and blocking configuration unchanged.
2. Compare the current offers and exclusions on two or three portal websites only after deciding what to buy. TopCashback and BeFrugal are sensible first checks for cash; include Rakuten when its rate or points option is better.
3. Open the dedicated `Shopping` profile in Zen. Sign in to exactly one selected cashback site, accept the cookies needed for the trip, and activate its website offer. Use Firefox as a fallback if a portal or merchant fails in Zen.
4. Complete the purchase in the merchant tab that the portal opened. Do not visit another deal portal, use a competing extension, clean the URL, switch to private browsing, clear state, or introduce an unlisted coupon before checkout finishes.
5. Save the order confirmation and shopping-trip/click identifier. Check the expected posting window and file a claim before that provider's deadline.
6. Close the shopping profile after the order and activation evidence are secure. Clear its state deliberately after checkout rather than risking an accidental mid-checkout close that deletes the active session.

This keeps Rakuten, TopCashback, BeFrugal, or RetailMeNot from observing unrelated daily browsing through an all-sites extension. The chosen provider and merchant still receive the account, device/network, cookie, and purchase data needed to attribute and validate that transaction. A separate profile limits retained browser state; it does not make an affiliate transaction anonymous.

### Portal website versus extension

The portal-only route has the smaller security and privacy surface. It avoids persistent all-site extension privileges and makes the beginning of tracking an explicit user action. The cost is a few manual steps and a greater need to verify that the trip activated. TopCashback, BeFrugal, Rakuten, RetailMeNot, and current Honey terms all support an extension-free route.

The user chose Rakuten's extension for the Shopping profile after reviewing and accepting its collection and Zen-compatibility risks. The profile boundary keeps the extension out of ordinary Zen browsing. It does not stop Rakuten from observing shopping and checkout activity inside that profile.

An extension is justified only if missed activation reminders or coupon automation are worth its browsing and checkout access. If one is used, install exactly one cashback extension in the shopping profile, keep that profile closed outside transactions, and remove the extension when it is no longer useful. Capital One Shopping belongs in this category because its desktop earning flow is extension-centered. Its gift-card-only reward and broad collection make that tradeoff hard to justify for occasional use.

### Firefox versus Zen

There are two defensible choices. Firefox is the compatibility-first choice because it is named by every provider that publishes browser support, while Zen is never named and Rakuten expressly declines to guarantee third-party browsers. A separate Zen profile is the UX-first choice because Zen is the user's main browser and it avoids making Firefox feel like a special-purpose purchasing application.

Zen inherits Firefox's profile model and documents profile management through `about:profiles`. A full profile, unlike a workspace or container, is the right boundary because containers separate cookies and sessions but not history or extensions. [Zen profile guide](https://docs.zen-browser.app/guides/manage-profiles), [Zen workspace and container limitations](https://docs.zen-browser.app/user-manual/workspaces), [Mozilla profile isolation](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/profile-management)

For this user's stated priorities, the recommended boundary is a non-default Zen `Shopping` profile with Firefox retained as an ordinary browser and compatibility fallback. A portal-only flow is still the lower-risk default. The configured exception is Rakuten's extension, which the user explicitly chose after accepting its privacy and Zen-compatibility risks. The first use should be a low-value purchase, and the portal's recorded trip should be checked promptly.

The current Nix configuration provides a non-default Zen `Shopping` profile. It uses Standard content blocking for checkout compatibility, disables form fill and Firefox Sync, and clears transaction state on clean shutdown. Cashback Monitor is its start page. Rakuten is bootstrapped only into that profile, then Zen owns its updates. See [`modules/home/browsers/zen/shopping.nix`](../modules/home/browsers/zen/shopping.nix) and the personal [`zen-shopping.nix`](../homes/macbook-pro-m4/local/browsers/zen-shopping.nix).

Firefox enterprise `ExtensionSettings` are application-wide even though Firefox stores manually installed add-ons per profile. In this repository, the [policy generator](../lib/browser/extensions.nix) blocks undeclared extensions, while the [personal extension catalog](../homes/shared/local/browsers/extensions.nix) permits Firefox-specific entries as `allowed` and requires Bitwarden globally. Therefore:

- The website-only workflow needs no extension-policy change.
- Application policy allows Rakuten but does not install it globally. Rycee's generated Firefox add-on catalog supplies the AMO ID and signed package through the flake lock. A generic profile bootstrap copies that package only when the Shopping profile does not already have it, which lets Zen update the writable installed copy.
- Do not force-install a cashback extension. That would put it in every profile of the selected browser and defeat the intended boundary.
- Bitwarden remains the only globally required extension and provides clear checkout value without creating affiliate-attribution competition.

## Decision guide

| Priority | Best first check | Reason and caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash with minimal payout friction | TopCashback website | No minimum for ACH/PayPal/Venmo and no extension required. Payability still depends on retailer confirmation. |
| Similar cash alternative | BeFrugal website | One-cent electronic payout minimum and generally next-business-day requested payouts after approval. It often asks for a less privacy-friendly tracking setup. |
| Amex or Built points | Rakuten website | Unique redemption options and familiar portal flow. Cash is quarterly with a $5.01 minimum. |
| PayPal checkout credit | PayPal or Honey website offer | Extension-free options exist. Rewards are points and, since Aug 1, 2026, cannot be withdrawn as cash. |
| One-off RetailMeNot promotion | RetailMeNot website | Website activation and fast post-approval redemption. Avoid leaving a small balance idle because the inactivity fee starts after 12 months without a credited transaction. |
| Capital One Shopping-only deal | Dedicated Firefox shopping profile with its extension | Use only when the offer clearly beats alternatives. Gift-card-only rewards, broad data access, and extension dependence make it the highest-cost privacy tradeoff here. |

The practical rule is to choose per purchase, not per brand loyalty, while exposing only the chosen provider to that purchase session.
